Why these books exist

Years of paying the ADHD tax. Of trying every productivity system that wasn't built for me. Of masking my way through rooms and realising afterwards I had nothing left. Common Questions

Where to start, and what these books are.

The questions readers ask most often — about which book to pick up first, how the series fit together, and what these books are and aren't.

Which J.M. Struijk book should I start with?

Start with the book that matches the problem costing you the most right now. If it's money — impulsive spending, forgotten bills, subscriptions you never cancelled — start with The ADHD Tax. If it's your head — mental loops, intrusive thoughts, the 2 a.m. spiral — start with The ADHD Overthinking Reset. If you were diagnosed as an adult and are still making sense of it, start with The Late-Diagnosed ADHD Workbook or The Late-Diagnosed Autistic Workbook. Every book stands alone, so there's no wrong entry point.

Do I have to read the series in order?

No. The ADHD Reset Series is numbered — Overthinking is Book 1, Eating is Book 2, Decision is Book 3 — but each one is self-contained and assumes you haven't read the others. The same is true of the ADHD Money Mastery Series. Read them in whatever order your life demands.

Are these books for ADHD, autism, or both?

Both, depending on the title. The ADHD Money Mastery and ADHD Reset series are written for ADHD adults. The Late-Diagnosed Autistic Workbook is written for autistic adults. The Late-Diagnosed ADHD Workbook and The ADHD Burnout Recovery Guide are written with AuDHD readers in mind throughout. J.M. is both ADHD and autistic, so the overlap is never an afterthought.

What does AuDHD mean?

AuDHD is an informal term for someone who is both autistic and ADHD. The two frequently co-occur, and the combination often produces conflicting needs — a craving for novelty alongside a need for routine, for example — that advice written for either condition alone tends to miss.

What's the difference between the Reset Series and the Late-Diagnosed Workbooks?

The Reset Series targets one specific stuck behaviour at a time — overthinking, eating, deciding — and gives you a way out of that loop. The Late-Diagnosed Workbooks are broader and more reflective: they're for adults who found out late and are reinterpreting their whole history, with prompts and exercises rather than a single fix.

Is the ADHD Survival Kit actually free?

Yes. It's a free printable PDF of tools that still work on a bad week, sent to your inbox. No payment, no trial, and you can unsubscribe in one click.

What formats are the books available in, and where can I buy them?

All titles are on Amazon. Most are available as Kindle ebooks, paperbacks and hardcovers — the format options live on each book's Amazon page. You can browse everything on the Amazon author page. This site doesn't sell directly.

Are these books a substitute for diagnosis or treatment?

No. They're practical self-help workbooks written from lived experience and current research, not clinical texts. They don't diagnose anything and they're not a replacement for working with a doctor, therapist, or ADHD coach. If you're wondering whether you're ADHD or autistic, these books may help you put words to it — but a professional assessment is a different thing, and worth having.

Who is J.M. Struijk?

An independent author who is autistic with ADHD, originally diagnosed with Asperger's, and a single father who raised a child with autism and ADHD. He writes the practical manuals he wanted on his own shelf. Books are published through JMS Media Group LLC, a small Wyoming company J.M. runs personally — no course, no conference circuit. There are eleven titles in print across three series.
