If you need help right now

If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number. These services offer free, confidential support from trained people when things feel unmanageable.

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis LifelineUnited States

    Call, text or chat 988

    Free, confidential support 24 hours a day from trained crisis counsellors, by phone, text or online chat.

  • 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis HelplineCanada

    Call or text 9-8-8

    Free support for anyone thinking about suicide, available 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

  • SamaritansUK and Ireland

    Call 116 123 for free

    A listening service for anyone who is struggling, whatever the reason.

  • LifelineAustralia

    Call 13 11 14

    Crisis support and suicide prevention, with phone, text and online options.

  • 1737, Need to talk?New Zealand

    Call or text 1737

    Free, confidential support around the clock from trained counsellors for anyone in New Zealand.

  • Find A HelplineInternational

    A directory of verified crisis helplines in other countries, searchable by location and topic.

ADHD organisations

National charities and nonprofits for adults with ADHD. Most offer free information, and several run peer support groups, helplines or directories of professionals.

  • ADDA, Attention Deficit Disorder AssociationInternational

    An organisation focused specifically on adults with ADHD, offering virtual support groups, webinars, a professional directory, and body doubling sessions for members.

  • CHADDUnited States

    A US nonprofit that runs the National Resource Center on ADHD, with fact sheets, local chapters, online communities, and directories of ADHD professionals and centres.

  • ADHD UKUnited Kingdom

    A UK charity with information on getting diagnosed, including the NHS Right to Choose route, plus guidance on work and education and online support groups.

  • CADDAC, Centre for ADHD Awareness CanadaCanada

    A national charity working on ADHD awareness, education and advocacy across Canada.

  • ADHD AustraliaAustralia

    A national organisation providing ADHD information, resources and community support for individuals, families, educators and employers.

Autism organisations

Several of these are run by autistic people themselves, which tends to show in how they write about autism: as a difference to understand and accommodate rather than a problem to fix.

  • Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN)United States

    A nonprofit run by and for autistic people, working on policy and self-advocacy, with resources on health care, education, community living and accessibility.

  • Autistic Women & Nonbinary Network (AWN)United States

    A nonprofit focused on autistic women, girls, nonbinary and trans people, working from a neurodiversity and disability justice perspective.

  • National Autistic SocietyUnited Kingdom

    The UK's main autism charity, with advice across fourteen topic areas from diagnosis and employment to money and relationships, an online community, and an autism services directory.

  • Reframing AutismAustralia

    An autistic-led charity with a free Autistic Welcome Pack and a free Autism Essentials course, plus resources for people who are newly identified or still questioning.

  • AutisticaUnited Kingdom

    A UK autism research and campaigning charity focused on helping autistic people live long, healthy lives.

Getting assessed as an adult

Official information on how adult assessment works. Routes, waits and costs differ a great deal between countries, so start with the source for where you live.

  • NHS: ADHD in adultsUnited Kingdom

    How adult ADHD shows up and how assessment works on the NHS, starting with an appointment with a GP.

  • NHS: Autism assessmentsUnited Kingdom

    What an NHS autism assessment involves and how to ask for one as an adult.

  • ADHD UK: Right to ChooseEngland

    In England you have a legal right to choose who carries out your NHS ADHD or autism assessment, which can shorten long local waits. This guide explains how to ask your GP.

  • NICE guideline NG87: ADHDUnited Kingdom

    The clinical guideline UK services follow for diagnosing and managing ADHD, including in adults.

  • NICE guideline CG142: Autism in adultsUnited Kingdom

    The clinical guideline for recognising, referring and diagnosing autism in adults in the UK.

  • NIMH: ADHDUnited States

    Plain-language information on ADHD from the US National Institute of Mental Health.

  • NIMH: Autism spectrum disorderUnited States

    Plain-language information on autism from the US National Institute of Mental Health.

Self-screening questionnaires

Screeners can tell you whether an assessment is worth pursuing. They cannot diagnose you, and both sites below say so plainly. My article on what online ADHD and autism tests can tell you covers how to read the results.

  • ADDA Adult ADHD TestInternational

    A short online self-screener based on the World Health Organization's Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS). ADDA describes it as a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

  • Embrace Autism testsInternational

    Free online versions of widely used autism questionnaires, including the AQ, RAADS-R and the CAT-Q, which measures camouflaging. The site notes its content is not medical advice.

Related: Online ADHD and autism tests: what they can and cannot tell you

Finding a professional

Directories for finding clinicians, therapists and coaches who work with ADHD and autistic adults.

  • CHADD Professional DirectoryUnited States

    A searchable directory of professionals and service providers working with ADHD.

  • ADHD Coaches OrganizationInternational

    The professional association for ADHD coaches, with a searchable directory of coaches.

  • Psychology Today therapist directoryUnited States

    A large directory of therapists and counsellors, searchable by location, speciality and insurance.

Peer support and body doubling

Talking to people who already understand saves a great deal of explaining. Virtual body doubling is included here because, for a lot of ADHD adults, other people's presence is what makes starting possible.

  • Adult ADHD community (CHADD on HealthUnlocked)International

    A free online peer community for adults with ADHD, run with CHADD.

  • Women with ADHD community (CHADD on HealthUnlocked)International

    A free online peer community for women with ADHD, run with CHADD.

  • FocusmateInternational

    Virtual body doubling: short video sessions where you and another person each work on your own task. There is a free tier.

Related: Body doubling: why ADHD tasks get easier when someone else is in the room

Work and reasonable adjustments

Your rights at work depend on where you live and who employs you. These are the official and expert sources, and they are a better guide than anything general I could write.

  • Job Accommodation Network (JAN)United States

    Free, expert and confidential guidance on workplace accommodations, for employees and employers.

  • JAN: ADHD accommodation ideasUnited States

    Practical workplace accommodation ideas for ADHD, organised by the difficulty they address.

  • JAN: Autism accommodation ideasUnited States

    Practical workplace accommodation ideas for autistic employees.

  • EEOC: Disability-related resourcesUnited States

    The US federal agency that enforces disability discrimination law at work. Its employment protections apply to employers with 15 or more employees.

  • Acas: Reasonable adjustments at workUnited Kingdom

    What reasonable adjustments are and when the Equality Act 2010 requires an employer to make them.

  • Access to WorkEngland, Scotland and Wales

    A UK government scheme that can fund support at work for disabled people and people with health conditions. Northern Ireland has a separate system.

Related: ADHD at work: what goes wrong, what helps, and asking for adjustments

Free debt and money advice

Not-for-profit services that will look at your situation for free. If anyone wants a fee to manage your debt, try one of these first.

  • NFCC, National Foundation for Credit CounselingUnited States

    A network of non-profit credit counselling agencies that can help you work out a plan for your debt.

  • StepChangeUnited Kingdom

    A UK debt charity offering free debt advice and fee-free debt management.

  • National DebtlineEngland and Wales

    Free, confidential debt advice by phone, online chat or self-help tools.

  • MABS, Money Advice and Budgeting ServiceIreland

    Free, confidential help with money, debt and budgeting for people in Ireland.

  • National Debt HelplineAustralia

    Free, confidential, not-for-profit financial counselling and step-by-step guides.

  • Credit Counselling CanadaCanada

    A national association of not-for-profit credit counselling agencies.

Related: ADHD and debt: why payoff plans keep failing, and what to do instead

I write from lived experience as a late-diagnosed ADHD and autistic adult, not as a clinician. Nothing here diagnoses anything or replaces working with a doctor, therapist, or ADHD coach. If something in your life has become unmanageable, please talk to someone qualified.
