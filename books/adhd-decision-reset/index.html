The ADHD Decision Reset

Break Analysis Paralysis, Make Choices Faster, and Stop Overthinking Everything

Everyone else at the table has ordered. You have been looking at the menu for ten minutes and still cannot decide. It is rarely about the food.

ADHD changes how decisions get processed, which is why ordinary choices can feel overwhelming and why "just pick one" does not help. This book gives you fixed protocols that turn decisions into something you can finish in two to ten minutes. It is the third book in the ADHD Reset Series.

Who it's for

Anyone with ADHD who has spent twenty minutes choosing a meal, weeks researching a purchase without buying, or years "figuring out" a career. Also anyone who freezes in front of a to-do list because every item looks equally urgent.

What's inside

The core of the book is three protocols matched to the size of the decision:

  • The 2-Minute Decision Protocol for everyday choices like what to eat, wear or do.
  • The 5-Minute Decision Protocol for medium decisions such as purchases and plans.
  • The 10-Minute Decision Protocol for large life choices about work and relationships.

Around those sit the Emergency Decision Interrupt for when you are frozen, a method for removing most routine daily decisions altogether, and a "good enough" standard for perfectionism paralysis. It works through specific traps: food, shopping, prioritising tasks, career and life decisions, and avoiding difficult conversations.

The goal is not to turn you into a decisive person. It is to make decisions fast enough to get on with your life.

Where it fits

The ADHD Overthinking Reset covers rumination more broadly, and The ADHD Eating Reset deals with the food version of this problem. The articles on decision paralysis and task paralysis go deeper on why choosing is so hard.

Common questions

Does it help with big decisions or only small ones?

Both. There is a separate protocol for large life decisions about career and relationships, as well as the shorter ones for everyday and medium choices.

What is the "good enough" standard?

It is the book's approach to perfectionism paralysis: choosing a level of quality that is sufficient instead of searching for the best possible option.

Is decision paralysis an ADHD thing?

Difficulty making decisions is commonly reported by adults with ADHD and is linked to executive function. It also overlaps with anxiety, so if indecision is part of broader, persistent worry, it is worth discussing with a professional.
