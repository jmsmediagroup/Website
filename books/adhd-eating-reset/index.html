The ADHD Eating Reset

End Meal Paralysis, Stop Forgetting to Eat, and Build Food Habits Your ADHD Brain Will Actually Keep

Published 31 December 2025·96 pages·By J.M. Struijk

It is four in the afternoon and you have just realised you have not eaten. Not because you were skipping meals. You forgot. Now you are standing at the fridge, starving and irritable, unable to choose anything.

ADHD changes how you process hunger, time, decisions and reward, which is why generic food advice rarely sticks. This book has no meal plans and no diets. It is a set of tools for eating consistently, even imperfectly. It is the second book in the ADHD Reset Series.

Who it's for

  • You forget to eat, then crash hard when you finally remember.
  • You spend twenty minutes deciding what to eat and end up with nothing.
  • You eat the same safe foods on repeat because deciding is exhausting.
  • You have tried meal planning and abandoned it by Wednesday.
  • You sometimes eat crackers for dinner and feel terrible about it.

What's inside

  • The "Just Eat Something" Protocol, which breaks meal paralysis in about 90 seconds.
  • The Decision Funnel, for ending "what should I eat?" spirals.
  • The Cooking Activation System, for days when cooking feels impossible.
  • How to interrupt dopamine-driven eating without shame or restriction.
  • How to stop forgetting to eat, and what to do when you forget anyway.
  • Strategies for grocery shopping, eating with other people, and emotional eating.

The aim is eating consistently, even if it is the same thing every day.

Where it fits

This is one book in the Reset Series, which takes on one stuck pattern at a time. The ADHD Overthinking Reset covers mental loops and The ADHD Decision Reset covers analysis paralysis, which overlaps heavily with food decisions. The article on meal paralysis is a good place to start.

Common questions

Is this a diet book?

No. There are no diets and no meal plans. It is about the executive function side of eating: remembering, deciding and getting started.

Is it okay to eat the same foods every day?

The book treats repetitive safe foods as a reasonable strategy rather than a failure. For specific nutritional questions, a doctor or dietitian is the right person to ask.

Does it help with forgetting to eat?

Yes, that is one of its main subjects, along with what to do when you have already forgotten and are past the point of making good decisions.
