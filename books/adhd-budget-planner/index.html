The ADHD Budget Planner

The Undated 12-Month Money Management System for Adults with ADHD

Gives you twelve undated monthly plans, a bill due-date tracker and weekly money check-ins, to keep a budget going after setup.

Get the book What is inside
Published August 8, 2026PaperbackBy J.M. Struijk

Dated planners have a built-in failure point: the first skipped week. For an ADHD brain that week always arrives, and then the planner goes in a drawer with the guilt attached.

This one is undated. You name the months yourself, you can start in any of them, and missing one does not break anything.

Who it's for

Adults with ADHD who have abandoned planners before and want a money system that waits for them when they come back. It also suits anyone who has built a budget system and needs somewhere to keep it running year after year.

What's inside

It opens with a one-hour Setup section, which does most of the heavy lifting:

  • A Bills Master List that gets every due date out of your head and onto paper.
  • The Subscription Hunt, for finding charges you forgot about and reclaiming that money.
  • The Autopilot Checklist, covering autopay, moved due dates and payment confirmations.

Then there are twelve identical undated months. Each has a one-page Money Map of the month's budget, a bill due-date tracker, four 10-minute weekly check-ins, an ADHD Tax log that records late fees, impulse buys and forgotten subscriptions, and a month review. Quarterly pit stops use a one-change rule to stop you rebuilding the whole system instead of using it. The Money Map is for the plan: there is no separate log for day-to-day spending and no page for annual or irregular bills, so keep those somewhere else if you track them.

For the hard moments there is a Missed-Payment page that scripts the call asking for a fee to be waived, a Restart page for when you find the planner after weeks away, and colourable progress trackers.

Where it fits

The planner is the long-term home for a system. The ADHD Budget Workbook is the guided 12-week reset that builds it. The ADHD Debt Payoff Workbook handles debt specifically, and The ADHD Tax explains the patterns all of it is designed around.

The planner and the Debt Payoff Workbook share many page designs, including the weekly check-in, the month review and the restart pages. About 45 of the workbook's 71 pages are versions of pages in this planner. If you want to track all your money for a year, choose the planner. If paying off debt is the job, choose the workbook. Buying both gets you two versions of the same weekly check-in.

If you would like to try the approach on a screen first, the free ADHD Budget Template is built on the planner's bills list, money map, weekly check-in and ADHD Tax log, and downloads for Excel and Google Sheets.

Common questions

Why is the planner undated?

Because the skipped week is where dated planners fail. Undated months mean you can start any time, miss a month, and continue without blank pages reminding you of the gap.

How long does setup take?

The Setup section is designed to take about an hour. After that, each week needs a 10-minute check-in.

Do I need the Budget Workbook first?

No, the planner works on its own. The workbook is useful if you want a guided program to build the system before you start maintaining it in the planner.
