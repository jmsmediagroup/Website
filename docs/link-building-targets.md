# Link targets for jmstruijk.com

Researched 22 October 2026. Every URL below was opened and checked on that date.

## What the research found first

There are no independent mentions of J.M. Struijk or the books anywhere on the
web. An extended search for reviews, lists or citations returned only Amazon
listings and one Goodreads page. So there are no unlinked mentions to convert,
and the author entity is effectively undefined outside Amazon.

That changes the order of work. Before any outreach, claim the profiles that
already exist and are currently pointing nowhere.

A second finding worth knowing: most "useful links" pages in this niche do not
exist. Seven plausible URLs on AADD-UK, ADDISS, ADHD UK and Autistica all
returned 404. Resource-page link building, which general SEO advice treats as
the easy win, has thin pickings here. The realistic routes are profiles,
contributed writing, and being quotable to journalists.

## The best opportunities, ranked

Researched 22 October 2026. Ranked by value multiplied by how achievable it
actually is for a site with no history.

### Why podcasts come first

Show notes link to the guest's site on essentially every episode. The link is
editorial, it sits in relevant content, the audience is exactly right, and
podcasters need guests continuously, which inverts the usual dynamic: you are
supplying something they need rather than asking for a favour. For a credible
author with a distinctive angle this has the best hit rate of anything here.

One episode also produces more than a link: an audio archive, show notes, a
social post from the host, and often a newsletter mention.

### The angle that makes the pitch work

ADHD and money is a genuinely thin niche. A search for who covers it found one
competing book, ADHD Money by Tina Mathams (Wiley, 2025), and no journalists
who write the beat at all.

Almost every ADHD podcast has covered focus, procrastination and productivity
to exhaustion. Very few have done money properly, and fewer still have had a
guest who is both ADHD and autistic talking about it from the inside. Pitch
the money angle, not "I wrote some books".

Specifics that make an episode rather than a plug: what the ADHD tax is and
where it actually goes, why budgeting advice fails this audience, late fees as
an executive function problem rather than a discipline one, and the shame loop
that keeps the number unknown.

### What is not worth the time

Book roundup posts mostly link to Amazon rather than to an author site.
Competitor outreach, meaning the other ADHD money author, is unlikely to
convert. Blog comment links, paid placements and directory submissions are
covered under what not to do.

## Tier 2: contributed writing. Best link quality, most effort.

A contextual link from a relevant article is worth far more than any directory
listing, and these outlets reach exactly the right readers. Pitch an original
piece, not a reprint, and do not pitch anything that reads as an advert.

## Tier 5: communities. Traffic, not links.

Reddit and similar communities are nofollow and strict about self-promotion,
and dropping links will get you banned rather than read. Treat them as places
to be useful and to learn what people actually ask, which feeds the writing.

If something of yours is genuinely the best answer to a question, link it once,
in context, having already participated properly. Read each community's rules
first.

## Form submissions: what actually exists

Checked 22 October 2026 by fetching each page and looking for a real `<form>`
element, then reading what the form is for and what it costs. Worth doing,
because most pages that sound like a submission form are not one.

### The one that qualifies

**The Neurodiversity Directory** — https://neurodiversity.directory/add-listing/

Free, not-for-profit, and curated rather than paid. Its own words: "the
independent, not-for-profit directory of vetted neurodiversity support; free to
use, free to list, curated not paid." Every listing is reviewed before it goes
live, which can take up to a week.

The form asks for: your name, your email, your relationship to the listing, the
listing name, website, description, category, city, country and a consent box.

One honest problem. The fourteen categories are coaching, therapy, apps,
assessments, charity, community, consulting, education, workplace, speaker,
training, sensory clothing, sensory solutions and sensory toys. There is no
books, author or resources category.

The only accurate fit is **Apps**, submitted as the two free web tools rather
than as the book catalogue. Both already carry `WebApplication` structured data,
both are free, and neither needs a sign-up, so the listing would be truthful.
Submitting an author site under a service category would not be, and a vetted
directory is exactly the kind that rejects it.

UK-leaning, so the city and country fields matter.

### Paid, so excluded

**The ADDitude Directory** — https://directory.additudemag.com/

This is advertising, not a listing. The rate card at `/advertise.php` shows
$45, $75, $175, $245 and $325 tiers under "Advertise in the ADDitude
Directory". Its categories are treatment professionals, educational resources,
coaches and organizers, and camps, none of which fits an author anyway.

Paid links have to be marked `sponsored` or `nofollow`, so this buys visibility
rather than ranking, and it falls under the no-paid-links rule below. Skip it.

### Free, but there is nothing to submit

**Neurodiversity UK events** — https://neurodiversityuk.co.uk/add-your-event/

A genuine free form, and it explicitly offers a link: "Free Listing: it's free
to add your event to our directory. Website Traffic: we can link to your
website." It only takes events. Worth remembering if there is ever a talk,
workshop or launch to list.

**National Autistic Society directory** — https://www.autism.org.uk/directory

Live, but its two forms are both search boxes, one for the site and one for
postcode lookups. It is the Autism Services Directory: a database of services
for autistic people in the UK. An author site is not a service and would not
qualify.

### A real form, but no link: The Spectrum

**https://www.autism.org.uk/advice-and-guidance/the-spectrum/submit-work-and-our-rules**
Form: **.../submit-work-and-our-rules/form**

Found while checking the NAS directory, and worth recording properly because
the eligibility is a better fit than anything else here. The Spectrum is the
National Autistic Society's quarterly magazine, "produced by and for autistic
adults", open to "submissions on any topic from autistic people who are over
18". Print and online, four issues a year, in January, April, July and October.
The October 2026 theme is "Fear and fiction: the stories told about autistic
people". Unthemed submissions are accepted too.

The form is real, though rendered by JavaScript so it does not appear in the
page source. It asks for a WeTransfer link to the file, a consent box, title,
first name, last name, email and phone. Submissions go as Word, PDF or JPEG,
and they will not accept a file emailed without the permissions form.

**It is not a link opportunity, and the reason is worth stating plainly.**
Contributors are credited by first name only, deliberately, to protect privacy.
No full name, no byline, no URL. The National Autistic Society also holds
copyright unless you tick the box to retain it, and content may be syndicated
to other organisations.

So this earns nothing for SEO. What it earns is a credit in a respected
autistic-led publication and an audience of exactly the right readers. If that
is worth an afternoon's writing, it is a good use of one. If the goal is
strictly links, skip it. Tick the retain-copyright box either way, so the piece
can be reused later.

The same applies to every regional database found, which were Commonwealth
Autism in Virginia, the Autism Society of North Carolina, the Minnesota Autism
Resource Portal and the Washington Autism Alliance. All are scoped to local
service providers.

### Contact forms are not listings

Several relevant sites have working contact forms: NeuroClastic, TotallyADD,
Neurodivergent Rebel, Neurodivergent Insights. A contact form is a way to send
the pitch described above, not a place that lists you. Use them for that, and
expect the same roughly one-in-ten hit rate as any other outreach.

**ADDitude's own contributor route is email, not a form.** Its contact page
links to contributor guidelines which ask for a 500 to 800 word sample blog
post sent to submissions@additudemag.com. They publish "blog posts by parents,
adults with ADHD, educators, spouses, and experts", which is a category that
genuinely applies here, and they say plainly that they cannot reply
individually.

This is the highest-authority realistic route in this document. It is also the
one that needs actual writing rather than a form fill.

### Why the pickings are thin

Free, form-based, relevant directories barely exist in this niche. That is
consistent with the earlier finding that seven plausible "useful links" pages
on AADD-UK, ADDISS, ADHD UK and Autistica all returned 404.

The niche is organised around service provision and clinical referral, so its
directories list clinicians, coaches and local services. An author with free
tools does not have an obvious slot. Which is why podcasts and contributed
writing rank above directory submissions: those routes judge what you have to
say rather than which category you fit.

## Verified dofollow routes, and the two list sites checked

Checked 23 October 2026. Two "free backlink sites" lists were assessed, then
the one useful technique in them was actually run. Follow status below was
confirmed by reading the `rel` attribute in published pages, not by trusting
any site's claim about itself.

### The two lists

**sites.google.com/view/free-backlink-sites-list-2026** contains no list.
5,371 characters of generic prose, eight category headings with nothing
underneath them, no site named anywhere except Quora, Reddit and LinkedIn in
passing, and exactly one outbound link, to the author's own consultancy with an
invitation to email him. There is nothing on the page to act on.

**ayrank.com/blog/free-backlink-sites** is a genuine 70-entry list and more
honest than most. Its own summary says "the highest-value free backlinks are
not directories" and "directories help with citation consistency, not
rankings", which is correct. It also refuses to invent Domain Rating figures,
and refuses to reproduce the usual .edu guestbook-spam list.

Almost none of it applies to this site, for reasons that are about eligibility
rather than quality:

| Category | Why it does not apply here |
| --- | --- |
| Local and business directories | No premises, no address, no phone. Google Business Profile needs a real location or service area, so claiming one would be false and risks suspension. All nofollow regardless. |
| Profile links | All nofollow. Already covered by `content/profiles.yaml`, where the point is entity confirmation through sameAs, not ranking. |
| Web 2.0 (WordPress.com, Blogger, Wix, GitHub Pages) | These are the dofollow ones, and they are the trap. See below. |
| Review directories (G2, Capterra, Crunchbase) | B2B and SaaS vendor listings. Not eligible. |
| Forums | Nofollow and post-count gated. Two of the named ones are marketing forums that would be a poor neighbourhood for a health-adjacent author name. |
| Document sharing (SlideShare, Scribd, Issuu) | Nofollow, and hosting the PDF here is better than giving a copy away. |
| Creative portfolios (Behance, Dribbble) | No visual design work to show. |
| Stock-photo attribution | Genuinely novel, but nofollow by nature and a trickle. Needs original photography first. |
| Journalist platforms | Already Tier 4 above, and the strongest real dofollow source, because the link inherits whatever the publication uses. |
| Guest posts (Search Engine Journal, Smashing, freeCodeCamp) | Dofollow, wrong niche entirely. |

### Why the easy dofollow ones are the trap

WordPress.com, Blogger, Wix and GitHub Pages do pass dofollow links, and they
are free and instant, which is why they head every list of this kind.

Building a second blog on one of them in order to link back here is a link
scheme, and the duplicate or thin content makes it worse rather than safer. The
ayrank article says so itself: a thin property on these platforms "reads as a
doorway page and gets treated as spam". There are already 35 real articles on
this domain. A satellite copy of them helps nothing and risks the thing it is
meant to promote.

The general rule worth keeping: the follow attribute is exactly what sites
withhold from anything that did not require editorial judgement. So "easy" and
"dofollow" rarely co-occur, and where they do, it is usually because the
platform has given up on policing it.

**Autism Parenting Magazine** — https://www.autismparentingmagazine.com/writingsubmissions/

Free. "Authors own all rights to their work", contributors "are encouraged to

**Special Needs USA** — https://specialneedsusa.com/write-for-us

### The honest summary

Every verified free dofollow link available to this site requires writing an
article a human editor accepted. There is no directory shortcut, and the lists
that promise one either contain nothing, as with the first source, or say so
themselves, as with the second.

Ranked by value times achievability: LD Resource Foundation first, because the
dofollow promise is explicit and verified. The Art of Autism second, because
the fit is perfect and they pay. ADDitude third, highest authority and hardest.
Autism Parenting Magazine fourth, pending a check of whether that bio link
actually follows.

## What not to do

- No paid links, link exchanges, or directory blasts. These violate search
  engine spam policies and can get the site penalised, which would undo
  everything built so far.
- No comment or forum link drops.
- No mass outreach. Ten researched emails beat two hundred templated ones, and
  the templated ones damage the name.

## Realistic expectations

Cold outreach in a niche like this converts at roughly one in ten, often less.
Ten good links in the first year would be a solid result for a new site. The
Tier 1 profiles are the exception: those are near-certain and take an afternoon.

Do not expect movement in search rankings for months. That is normal and is not
a sign anything is wrong.

## Tracking

Keep a simple sheet: target, URL, date contacted, response, outcome. Do not
contact anyone twice. One follow-up after two weeks is acceptable; a second is
not.
