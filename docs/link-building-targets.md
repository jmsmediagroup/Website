# Link targets for jmstruijk.com

Researched 22 October 2026. Every URL below was opened and checked on that date.

## What the research found first

There are no independent mentions of J.M. Struijk or the books anywhere on the
web. An extended search for reviews, lists or citations returned only Amazon
listings and one Goodreads page. So there are no unlinked mentions to convert,
and the author entity is effectively undefined outside Amazon.

That changes the order of work. Before any outreach, claim the profiles that
already exist and are currently pointing nowhere.

A second finding worth knowing: most "useful links" pages in this niche do not
exist. Seven plausible URLs on AADD-UK, ADDISS, ADHD UK and Autistica all
returned 404. Resource-page link building, which general SEO advice treats as
the easy win, has thin pickings here. The realistic routes are profiles,
contributed writing, and being quotable to journalists.

## Tier 1: profiles you control. Do these first.

Near-certain, free, and they take an afternoon between them. Most are nofollow,
which is fine. The point is establishing the entity and the referral traffic,
not passing authority.

### 1. Goodreads author profile (highest value here)

**https://www.goodreads.com/author/list/60916846.J_M_Struijk**

This page already exists and already has an audience: 30 works, 179 shelvings,
4 reviews. It is **unclaimed**. There is no Goodreads Author badge, no bio, no
photo and, most importantly, **no link to jmstruijk.com**.

Claim it through the Goodreads Author Program
(https://www.goodreads.com/author/program), then add the website, a bio, and
the author photo. This is the single highest-value action on this page, because
the page exists, ranks for the author name, and currently sends nobody to the
site.

### 2. Amazon Author Central

**https://author.amazon.com/**

Add jmstruijk.com to the author profile. Amazon author pages rank well for
author-name searches, and this is the obvious place readers look next.

### 3. BookBub author profile

**https://partners.bookbub.com/authors** (blocks automated checks; open it in a
browser)

Free author profile with a website field.

## Tier 2: contributed writing. Best link quality, most effort.

A contextual link from a relevant article is worth far more than any directory
listing, and these outlets reach exactly the right readers. Pitch an original
piece, not a reprint, and do not pitch anything that reads as an advert.

### NeuroClastic

**https://neuroclastic.com/** · contact: https://neuroclastic.com/contact/

Autistic-led, multi-author publication. Its existing topic pages cover PDA,
masking, meltdowns, alexithymia and executive function, which overlaps directly
with what is already on jmstruijk.com. Strong fit, and an autistic-authored
perspective is what they publish.

### NeuroSpark Health

**https://neurosparkhealth.com/** (403 to automated checks; live in a browser)

Written and curated entirely by autistic, ADHD and AuDHD writers, and the site
explicitly asks people who want to contribute a blog to get in touch. The most
openly contributor-friendly outlet found.

Not open submission, so treat these as relationship-building rather than a
submissions queue. Read them, reply thoughtfully, and only suggest a
collaboration or guest piece once there is an actual relationship.

## Tier 5: communities. Traffic, not links.

Reddit and similar communities are nofollow and strict about self-promotion,
and dropping links will get you banned rather than read. Treat them as places
to be useful and to learn what people actually ask, which feeds the writing.

If something of yours is genuinely the best answer to a question, link it once,
in context, having already participated properly. Read each community's rules
first.

## What the tools make possible

The ADHD Tax Calculator and the Subscription Audit are the most linkable things
on the site, more so than the books. They are free, need no sign-up, store
nothing, and nothing else quite like them exists: a search for free printable
ADHD worksheets returns scraped spam on hijacked university domains.

Lead outreach with the tools. "Here is a free calculator your readers can use"
is a reason to link. "Here is my book" is a reason to ignore the email.

## What not to do

- No paid links, link exchanges, or directory blasts. These violate search
  engine spam policies and can get the site penalised, which would undo
  everything built so far.
- No comment or forum link drops.
- No mass outreach. Ten researched emails beat two hundred templated ones, and
  the templated ones damage the name.

## Realistic expectations

Cold outreach in a niche like this converts at roughly one in ten, often less.
Ten good links in the first year would be a solid result for a new site. The
Tier 1 profiles are the exception: those are near-certain and take an afternoon.

Do not expect movement in search rankings for months. That is normal and is not
a sign anything is wrong.

## Tracking

Keep a simple sheet: target, URL, date contacted, response, outcome. Do not
contact anyone twice. One follow-up after two weeks is acceptable; a second is
not.
