# Keyword research, 30 October 2026

What to write next, and why. Built from real queries, checked against who
already ranks for them.

## Method

1. Google autocomplete for 37 seed topics, each with 11 modifiers (in adults,
   how to, why, vs, book, help, women, at work, meaning, reddit), plus 15
   question stems and a full alphabet run on the three money seeds. 478
   requests, 2,147 unique suggestions. Autocomplete only shows phrases people
   actually type, which makes it a demand signal without needing a paid tool.
2. Dropped anything off the site's rules or audience: medication, children and
   school, quizzes, and noise. 2,052 left; 1,458 of four words or more.
3. Matched each against every existing article's slug, title and keywords.
   About 544 are already well covered and 454 are clear gaps.
   `keyword-gaps-2026-10-30.csv` has the full list, gaps first.
4. Clustered the gaps by recurring two-word phrases, then searched the top
   clusters to see who ranks. A cluster is only worth writing for when the
   results are weak: forums, Substacks, spam, or nothing written for that exact
   question.

To repeat it, rerun the autocomplete pass every couple of months. Queries
that carry "reddit" are worth watching separately: people add it when the
normal results do not answer them.

## Parked, with reasons

- **"Do I have ADHD or am I just lazy."** Strong demand and a good fit for the
  voice, but the results are therapy platforms, telehealth firms and rehab
  centres with far more authority. Worth writing once the site has more links.
- **"Is it ADHD or bipolar / BPD / dementia / perimenopause", "autism or
  narcissism".** Clinical differential questions. They sit outside the site's
  non-clinical stance and are not worth the risk.

## Originally next, now done

1. **Impulse spending checklist.** "adhd impulse buying checklist", "how to stop
   impulse spending", "why do I spend money when I'm bored / sad / so fast".
   ADDitude and Understood rank, but the checklists that exist are gated PDFs or
   blog lists. A free interactive pause-before-buying checklist, in the style of
   the other tools, would be distinct. why-adhd-brains-overspend can carry the
   bored and sad spending questions as FAQ entries in the meantime.
2. **Paying bills on time.** "how to pay bills on time", "pay bills ahead of
   time". General queries with an ADHD angle nobody owns. Fits the ADHD tax and
   the Planner's due-date tracker.
3. **"Am I autistic or ADHD / or just weird".** Real and frequent, but the
   results are crowded and the subject needs care. Strengthen
   adhd-vs-autism-adults before writing anything new.
4. **Burnout books.** "autistic burnout book", "adhd burnout book" (already
   position 10 in Search Console). Mostly an authority problem; getting the book
   onto existing "best books on burnout" lists is outreach, not writing.

## What the Search Console data says about this site

It ranks on page one where nobody is competing and nowhere on contested head
terms ("stimming in adults" 94, "adhd task paralysis" 97). Mobile positions are
far better than desktop. Non-English markets rank on page one with tiny volumes.
That is the profile of a site with relevant content and little authority, which
is why specific long-tail pages and outreach both matter more than polishing
existing pages.
