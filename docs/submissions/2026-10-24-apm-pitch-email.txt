Your guidelines suggest checking a topic with you before writing, so here is
the idea.

Working title: "I am autistic too, and I raised my autistic son alone"

I am an autistic adult with ADHD, originally diagnosed with Asperger's. I
raised a child with autism and ADHD as a single father. Most of what your
readers see is written by parents who are not themselves autistic, or by
professionals. I can write the view from inside both positions at once, which
I do not think you publish often.

What the piece would cover:

- What I spotted early, and why. Some of what later got formally assessed was
  obvious to me years before, because I had lived it myself. That cuts both
  ways: it also made me slow to flag things I had assumed were normal.
- Two autistic nervous systems in one house, with no third person in it. What
  happens when your child needs you at the exact moment your own capacity has
  run out, and there is nobody to hand over to.
- Where being autistic genuinely helped. Routine, directness, taking his
  explanations at face value rather than reading them as behaviour.
- What it cost, said plainly rather than redemptively.

Around 950 words, which keeps it inside your guidance for personal narratives.
Original, not published anywhere, and I would not include images of my son or
identifying detail about him.

One question on house style. Your guidelines use person-first phrasing, as in
"a child with autism". I am autistic and I write identity-first, as most
autistic adults I know do. I am happy to follow your house style if you would
rather, but I wanted to raise it rather than have it edited silently.

If the topic is already covered or not a fit, no problem at all.
