I write about ADHD and money, which is the one subject I could not find anywhere in your archive.

I am autistic with ADHD, originally diagnosed with Asperger's, and I have eleven books out for adults working out what a late diagnosis means. Most of them are about what ADHD costs people financially, which I call the ADHD tax: late fees on bills you could easily afford, subscriptions you meant to cancel months ago, buying things twice because you could not find the first one.

Almost every ADHD show has covered focus, procrastination and clutter thoroughly. Money gets covered far less, and it is where a lot of us lose the most, partly because the shame around it stops people ever adding the number up.

Three things I could talk about:

- Where the money actually goes, which is rarely impulse buys

- Why conventional budgeting advice fails this audience specifically, rather than people failing at budgeting

- The shame loop that keeps people from opening the banking app at all
