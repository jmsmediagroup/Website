It is four in the afternoon and you have just realised you have not eaten. Not because you were skipping meals. You forgot. Now you are standing at the fridge, starving and irritable, unable to choose anything.

ADHD changes how you process hunger, time, decisions and reward, which is why generic food advice rarely sticks. This book has no meal plans and no diets. It is a set of tools for eating consistently, even imperfectly. It is the second book in the ADHD Reset Series.

## Who it's for

- You forget to eat, then crash hard when you finally remember.
- You spend twenty minutes deciding what to eat and end up with nothing.
- You eat the same safe foods on repeat because deciding is exhausting.
- You have tried meal planning and abandoned it by Wednesday.
- You sometimes eat crackers for dinner and feel terrible about it.

## What's inside

- The ninety-second rescue, for when you are starving, standing at the fridge and unable to choose anything.
- The three-question funnel, with a two-minute timer, for ending "what should I eat?" spirals.
- A chapter on cooking when cooking feels impossible, including food for the nights you cannot cook at all.
- How to interrupt dopamine-driven eating without shame or restriction.
- Anchor meals tied to habits you already have, for forgetting to eat, and what to do when you forget anyway.
- Strategies for grocery shopping, eating with other people, and emotional eating.

The aim is eating consistently, even if it is the same thing every day.

## Common questions

### Is this a diet book?

No. There are no diets and no meal plans. It is about the executive function side of eating: remembering, deciding and getting started. It is also not written for eating disorders. The book says so at the start and points to specialist help, including the National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline in the US.

### Is it okay to eat the same foods every day?

The book treats repetitive safe foods as a reasonable strategy rather than a failure. For specific nutritional questions, a doctor or dietitian is the right person to ask. Talk to one too if your list of foods keeps shrinking, you are losing weight without trying, or new foods make you gag or panic. Those can be signs of ARFID, an eating disorder the book does not cover.

### Does it help with forgetting to eat?

Yes, that is one of its main subjects, along with what to do when you have already forgotten and are past the point of making good decisions.

### Does it cover appetite loss from ADHD medication?

Only in passing. Medication appears once, as a habit anchor (eat something when you take your morning dose), and the book does not discuss appetite loss on stimulants. If you are eating much less or losing weight on medication, tell your prescriber, and do not change the dose yourself. [The ADHD Medication Companion](/books/adhd-medication-companion/) has a daily log with an appetite column, which makes that conversation easier.
