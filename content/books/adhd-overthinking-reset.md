It is two in the morning and your brain is replaying a conversation from three days ago. You are tired, you know none of this matters at this hour, and the loop keeps running anyway.

The usual advice, to breathe or think positively or journal it out, tends to fall apart the moment you are actually inside the spiral. This book is a set of fast interventions for that moment, written for ADHD brains. It is the first book in the ADHD Reset Series.

## Who it's for

Anyone with ADHD who gets caught in mental loops: replaying conversations, spiralling over what someone meant, lying awake with racing thoughts. It is especially aimed at the spirals that hit ADHD brains hardest, including rejection sensitivity, money anxiety, productivity guilt, relationship overthinking and overstimulation.

## What's inside

The chapters are short and the tools are meant to be used in the moment:

- The 90-Second Interrupt, for breaking a loop before it takes over your day.
- The 5-Minute Grounding Protocol, for when overstimulation is pushing you toward shutdown.
- How to make decisions without needing perfect certainty first.
- Specific approaches for rejection sensitive dysphoria, money anxiety, productivity guilt and relationship overthinking.
- A chapter on sleep, food and stimulation, the boring levers that make spirals more or less likely.
- A prevention system that does not require a perfect morning routine.

The goal is not to stop spiralling forever. It is to catch rumination earlier, recover faster, and treat yourself more kindly along the way.

## Common questions

### How long is the book?

It is short on purpose, around 69 pages in print length on Amazon, with brief chapters you can use when your attention is limited.

### Does it cover rejection sensitive dysphoria?

Yes. RSD is one of the specific spirals the book addresses, alongside money anxiety, productivity guilt, relationship overthinking and overstimulation shutdowns.

### Is it about meditation?

No. The tools are quick, practical interrupts rather than meditation practices or long routines.

### Does it cover intrusive thoughts or OCD?

No. This edition is about everyday ADHD overthinking (replaying, worrying, spiraling) and does not cover intrusive thoughts or OCD. Unwanted thoughts that feel disturbing and keep coming back can be a sign of OCD, especially if you check, ask for reassurance or do rituals to get relief. Pushing those thoughts away and seeking reassurance can keep OCD going, so the tools in this book are the wrong fit for it. The established treatments are exposure and response prevention (ERP) with a trained therapist, and medication. The International OCD Foundation (iocdf.org) has a directory of therapists. Unwanted thoughts are not intentions. If you have urges or plans to hurt yourself or someone else, that is different: call or text 988 in the US, or 911 in an emergency, or find a [crisis line](/resources/#crisis) where you live.
