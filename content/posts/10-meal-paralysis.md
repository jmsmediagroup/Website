The fridge is full. You are hungry, genuinely hungry, the kind that has started to make you irritable. You open the door, look at everything in it, close the door, and eat a handful of dry cereal standing up.

It has two names in common use, meal paralysis and food paralysis, and they point at the same thing: being hungry, having food in the house, and being unable to start.

It is one of the stranger experiences to try explaining to someone who has not had it, because every element of the problem is apparently solved. There is food. You can cook. You want to eat. And none of that is enough.

## Key takeaways

- Eating is a multi-step executive task, which is where it breaks, rather than at wanting food.
- ADHD often blunts hunger signals until hunger arrives as an emergency.
- Deciding costs more than cooking does, so removing the decision helps most.
- A short rotation of repeated meals beats variety, and the guilt about that is misplaced.
- Food that requires nothing, available at all times, prevents the worst days.

## Why eating is harder than it looks

Making a meal is not one task. It is a sequence: notice you are hungry, decide what to have, check you have the components, choose a recipe, prepare, cook, serve, eat, clear up. Nine steps, several of which require decisions, with a delay between effort and reward.

That is a demanding chain for an ADHD brain even on a good day. On a bad day the chain breaks at step two and you eat cereal.

The hunger signal itself is often unreliable. Many people with ADHD describe interoception that runs quiet: you do not notice hunger building gradually, so there is no gentle prompt at the point where cooking would still be easy. Instead hunger arrives suddenly and at full volume, usually at the moment your capacity to do anything about it is lowest. Hungry is also the state in which decision-making is worst, which makes the whole thing self-defeating.

Stimulant medication can suppress appetite further, which is worth knowing and worth raising with a prescriber if you are losing weight or regularly eating nothing until evening.

Then there is the delay problem. Cooking is forty minutes of effort for a reward that arrives at the end. Something immediate and processed is thirty seconds for a reward now. For a brain that discounts delayed rewards steeply, that comparison is not close, and this has nothing to do with knowing which option is better.

## The decision is the expensive part

Most people assume the hard part is the cooking. In my experience the decision costs more.

Deciding what to eat is an open-ended choice with too many options, no clear criteria, and a mild penalty for getting it wrong. That is precisely the shape of problem that produces paralysis. Combine it with hunger, which has already degraded your decision-making, and you get the fridge scene.

This is why meal planning advice so often fails us. It asks you to make seven of these decisions at once, on a Sunday, which is more of the expensive thing rather than less. The people for whom meal planning works are generally people who did not find the decision hard in the first place.

> The fix is not better decisions about food. It is fewer decisions about food.

## What actually helps

Shrink the menu, hard. Pick four or five meals you can make nearly automatically and rotate them. Not for a week. Indefinitely. The variety you lose is worth less than the decisions you stop having to make, and the meals become cheap to execute because you stop needing to think about any of the steps.

People feel guilty about this. The guilt comes from a cultural idea that eating should be varied and interesting, which is a fine aspiration for people who are eating at all. Eating the same four dinners reliably is a substantially better outcome than cooking something interesting twice a week and having cereal the other five nights.

Keep food that requires nothing. Not as a nutritional strategy, as a floor. Things that can be eaten with no preparation, no decision, and no washing up, kept somewhere visible. On the worst days this is the difference between eating badly and not eating, and not eating makes everything else worse, including the capacity to make a better choice later.

Eat on a schedule rather than on hunger, if your hunger signal is unreliable. An alarm is a cruder instrument than appetite and considerably more dependable. Eating something small at a fixed time prevents the emergency-hunger state in which no good decision is available.

Use the convenience options deliberately instead of guiltily. Pre-chopped vegetables, frozen everything, the meal kit, the supermarket delivery. These remove steps from the chain, and steps are the actual problem. Paying slightly more to remove three steps from a nine-step process is a reasonable trade, not a moral failure.

And separate cooking from clearing up. The prospect of the washing up stops a lot of meals from happening. One pan, or a plate you accept will sit there until tomorrow, removes a blocker that has nothing to do with food.

## The shame part

There is usually a layer of embarrassment about this that makes it harder to address.

Feeding yourself is treated as the most basic form of adult competence. Being unable to do it reliably, while being perfectly capable of complex work, feels absurd, and it is the kind of thing people hide. I have met very few people who talk about this openly, and a great many who recognise it instantly when someone else does.

It helps to see it accurately. This is an executive function problem that happens to involve food. It is not a character failure, and it does not respond to being more disciplined about it, which is the approach most of us have already tried for years.

## When it is more than this

Worth saying plainly: if your eating is restricted by texture, colour, smell or a fear of particular foods to the point where it affects your health, that may be ARFID or another eating disorder rather than executive dysfunction, and those overlap with both autism and ADHD at higher rates.

Same if you are regularly not eating for long stretches, losing weight unintentionally, or the distress around food is significant. That is a conversation with a doctor. The strategies here are for a logistics problem, and they are the wrong tool for a clinical one.

## Common questions

### What does food paralysis mean?

Food paralysis, also called meal paralysis, means being hungry and having food available while being unable to decide what to eat or start preparing it. It is not fussiness, and it is not an absent appetite. The difficulty sits in deciding and starting, which is why it is better understood as an executive function problem than a problem with food.

### Why do I forget to eat all day and then binge at night?

Because unmet hunger does not disappear, it accumulates, and the evening is usually when demands finally drop and the signal breaks through. By then you are extremely hungry and your capacity to make considered choices is at its lowest. Eating something at fixed times during the day, whether or not you feel like it, is what most reliably interrupts this.

### Is forgetting to eat an ADHD symptom?

It is not a diagnostic criterion, and it is a very commonly reported experience among ADHD adults. It is usually attributed to a combination of reduced interoception, absorption in tasks, and appetite suppression from stimulant medication where that applies.

### Is it bad to eat the same meals every day?

Nutritionally it is worth having some variety across your rotation, and a doctor or dietitian can advise properly on that. Practically, a short rotation you actually eat beats a varied plan you abandon. If you want reassurance about the specifics of what you are eating, that is a good question for a professional rather than a reason to go back to deciding nightly.

### Does medication make this worse?

Stimulant medication commonly reduces appetite, which can make the forgetting worse even as it helps elsewhere. It is worth tracking and raising with your prescriber, particularly if you are losing weight or finding you eat nothing until the evening. Do not adjust medication on your own.

### What is the single most useful change?

Pick four meals and stop choosing. Almost everything else is downstream of removing the decision. [The ADHD Eating Reset](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GDH6R4DL) builds out the rotation and the no-preparation floor in more detail.
