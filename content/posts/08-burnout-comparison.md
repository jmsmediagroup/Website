From the outside these three look close to identical. Someone who was managing is no longer managing. They are exhausted, withdrawn, not answering messages, and unable to do things they could do easily six months ago.

From the inside they feel different, and more usefully, they respond to different things. Treating autistic burnout as depression wastes months. Treating depression as burnout can be worse than wasting months.

I am not a clinician and this is not a diagnostic tool. It is the distinction I wish someone had drawn for me years earlier, because I spent a long time applying the wrong remedy with great determination.

## Key takeaways

- Autistic burnout comes from sustained masking and sensory demand, and it needs reduced input.
- ADHD burnout usually follows a long stretch of effortful overcompensation, often after a crash.
- Depression is a medical condition that can occur alongside either, and it needs professional support.
- Burnout generally lifts when demands drop. Depression generally does not.
- The presence of anticipatory pleasure is one of the more useful signals, though none of this is a substitute for assessment.

## Autistic burnout

This one builds slowly, often over years, and then arrives as a collapse that seems disproportionate to whatever immediately preceded it.

The characteristic features are a loss of skills you previously had. Speech becomes harder, and some people lose it temporarily. Things you could tolerate become intolerable: lighting, noise, texture, the radio in someone else's car. The mask stops working, and social interactions you used to manage competently become impossible to perform.

Executive function drops sharply. Cooking, washing, replying to anything. The capacity simply is not available.

What causes it is sustained demand without adequate recovery. Masking all day. Managing sensory input you cannot control. Meeting social expectations that require constant translation. None of it registers as work, which is why the exhaustion seems to come from nowhere, and why people experiencing it frequently insist they have no reason to be this tired.

What helps is subtraction. Fewer demands, less sensory input, more time alone, and permission to stop masking. Recovery is often measured in months, not days, and people underestimate this consistently. Pushing through, which is the response most of us learned, makes it substantially worse.

## ADHD burnout

The shape here is different. Less of a slow build, more of a repeated cycle that eventually fails to reset.

The pattern is overcommitting during a good stretch, working at an unsustainable intensity to compensate for the periods where nothing got done, hitting a wall, recovering partially, and starting again slightly more depleted. Run that for long enough and the recovery stops happening.

It usually comes with a heavy load of guilt and a backlog. Unfinished projects, unanswered messages, things you promised during an optimistic phase and cannot now face. The shame is a load-bearing part of this one, because it drives the next round of overcompensation.

Interest-based attention stops working, which is the sign most people find alarming. The things that normally engage you, that you could always rely on to pull you out, do not. That loss of the one reliable fuel source is distinctive.

What helps is reducing the number of open loops, finishing or formally abandoning things, and rebuilding smaller commitments that you can actually meet. Rest alone often does not fix it, because the backlog is still generating pressure.

## Depression

Depression is a medical condition, and I want to be careful here, because the distinction matters most in this direction.

The feature most often described as distinguishing is pervasive low mood and anhedonia: the absence of pleasure, including anticipatory pleasure. With burnout, people usually still want things. They want to rest, want to see a particular person, look forward to something, and simply have no capacity to act. With depression, the wanting itself is frequently gone.

Depression also tends to carry a view of the self rather than of the circumstances. Burnout says I cannot keep doing this. Depression says I am worthless. The second is a different kind of statement.

And crucially, burnout generally improves when the demands come off. Depression often does not improve when circumstances improve, which is one of the cruellest things about it.

If there is any possibility of depression, that is a conversation with a doctor rather than a self-management project. If you are having thoughts of harming yourself, please contact emergency services or a crisis line where you are.

> Burnout tends to lift when the demands drop. If the demands dropped and nothing lifted, that is worth taking to a professional.

## They overlap

The reason this is genuinely difficult is that these rarely arrive one at a time.

Prolonged autistic burnout frequently produces depression. ADHD burnout and depression share a large portion of their presentation. Someone who is AuDHD can be in both kinds of burnout simultaneously, which is a particularly confusing place to be, because the two call for opposite responses: one wants less input, the other is starved of stimulation.

This is a strong argument against self-diagnosis as a final answer. It is a reasonable argument for using these distinctions as a way to describe your experience accurately to someone qualified, which tends to produce better help than arriving with no language for it.

## What to do with the distinction

The practical value is in choosing the first move.

If it looks like autistic burnout, subtract. Cancel what can be cancelled, reduce sensory load, stop masking where it is safe to, and plan in months.

If it looks like ADHD burnout, close loops. Finish or formally drop the open commitments, reduce what you agree to going forward, and address the backlog that is generating the pressure.

If anything suggests depression, particularly absent anticipatory pleasure, persistent hopelessness, or a change in how you see yourself rather than your circumstances, make an appointment. The self-management approaches are not a substitute and the delay is costly.

And if you are AuDHD and both are present, subtract first. The sensory and masking load has to come down before anything else is workable, and attempting the stimulation-based fixes while still overloaded generally fails.

## Common questions

### Is autistic burnout an official diagnosis?

No. It is a well-described experience in autistic communities and has been the subject of research, but it does not appear as a formal diagnostic category. That means clinicians vary a great deal in whether they recognise it, which is one reason many autistic adults are treated for depression first.

### How long does autistic burnout last?

Accounts vary widely, from weeks to well over a year, and the length depends heavily on whether the demands causing it actually come down. Recovery that keeps being interrupted by a return to the same load tends to extend indefinitely. This is one of the main reasons it is so often underestimated.

### Can you have burnout and depression at once?

Yes, and it is common, particularly when burnout has gone on a long time. Having one does not rule out the other, which is a good reason to involve a professional rather than deciding between them yourself.

### Why does rest not fix my ADHD burnout?

Usually because the pressure is not only depletion. Unfinished commitments, unanswered messages and unmet promises keep generating load whether you are resting or not. Many people find that closing or formally abandoning open loops does more than additional sleep.

### What is the first thing to do?

Reduce the demand that is actually causing it, which means working out which kind you are in. If you cannot tell, or if the low mood is persistent, start with a doctor. [The ADHD Burnout Recovery Guide](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GDLRFXQ3) covers the triage version of this in more depth.
