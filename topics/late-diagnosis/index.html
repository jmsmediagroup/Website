Late diagnosis: ADHD and autism in adulthood

For adults who suspect or have just found out they are ADHD or autistic: assessment, online tests, the first year, AuDHD, and why women are often missed.

Finding out as an adult that you are ADHD, autistic, or both tends to arrive as two things at once. There is relief, because a lifetime of feeling slightly out of step suddenly has a reason. And there is grief, for the years spent believing the problem was your character, and for the version of you who tried so hard at things that were never going to work the way they were being attempted.

This is the collection for that stage, whether you are still wondering, waiting for an assessment, or a few months past the answer. It covers how adult assessment works and how to prepare for it, what online tests can and cannot tell you, why so many women in particular were missed, and what the first year after finding out is usually like.

If you are only starting to wonder, read what online tests can tell you and how to get assessed as an adult. If you already know, the first year after an ADHD diagnosis and finding out you are autistic are where most people find themselves.

Key terms

Late diagnosis
Being identified as ADHD or autistic in adulthood rather than childhood.
AuDHD
An informal term for someone who is both autistic and ADHD.
Masking
Suppressing natural behaviour and performing expected behaviour to fit in: forcing eye contact, scripting conversations, hiding stimming, copying how other people act.
ADHD
Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, a lifelong neurodevelopmental condition that affects attention, impulse control, activity levels and the executive functions that organise behaviour.
Autism
A lifelong neurodevelopmental difference affecting social communication, sensory processing, and a preference for predictability and focused interests.

Organisations and help

Getting assessed as an adult

Official information on how adult assessment works. Routes, waits and costs differ a great deal between countries, so start with the source for where you live.

  • NHS: ADHD in adultsUnited Kingdom

    How adult ADHD shows up and how assessment works on the NHS, starting with an appointment with a GP.

  • NHS: Autism assessmentsUnited Kingdom

    What an NHS autism assessment involves and how to ask for one as an adult.

  • ADHD UK: Right to ChooseEngland

    In England you have a legal right to choose who carries out your NHS ADHD or autism assessment, which can shorten long local waits. This guide explains how to ask your GP.

  • NICE guideline NG87: ADHDUnited Kingdom

    The clinical guideline UK services follow for diagnosing and managing ADHD, including in adults.

  • NICE guideline CG142: Autism in adultsUnited Kingdom

    The clinical guideline for recognising, referring and diagnosing autism in adults in the UK.

  • NIMH: ADHDUnited States

    Plain-language information on ADHD from the US National Institute of Mental Health.

  • NIMH: Autism spectrum disorderUnited States

    Plain-language information on autism from the US National Institute of Mental Health.

Self-screening questionnaires

Screeners can tell you whether an assessment is worth pursuing. They cannot diagnose you, and both sites below say so plainly. My article on what online ADHD and autism tests can tell you covers how to read the results.

  • ADDA Adult ADHD TestInternational

    A short online self-screener based on the World Health Organization's Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS). ADDA describes it as a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

  • Embrace Autism testsInternational

    Free online versions of widely used autism questionnaires, including the AQ, RAADS-R and the CAT-Q, which measures camouflaging. The site notes its content is not medical advice.

ADHD organisations

National charities and nonprofits for adults with ADHD. Most offer free information, and several run peer support groups, helplines or directories of professionals.

  • ADDA, Attention Deficit Disorder AssociationInternational

    An organisation focused specifically on adults with ADHD, offering virtual support groups, webinars, a professional directory, and body doubling sessions for members.

  • CHADDUnited States

    A US nonprofit that runs the National Resource Center on ADHD, with fact sheets, local chapters, online communities, and directories of ADHD professionals and centres.

  • ADHD UKUnited Kingdom

    A UK charity with information on getting diagnosed, including the NHS Right to Choose route, plus guidance on work and education and online support groups.

  • CADDAC, Centre for ADHD Awareness CanadaCanada

    A national charity working on ADHD awareness, education and advocacy across Canada.

  • ADHD AustraliaAustralia

    A national organisation providing ADHD information, resources and community support for individuals, families, educators and employers.

Autism organisations

Several of these are run by autistic people themselves, which tends to show in how they write about autism: as a difference to understand and accommodate rather than a problem to fix.

  • Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN)United States

    A nonprofit run by and for autistic people, working on policy and self-advocacy, with resources on health care, education, community living and accessibility.

  • Autistic Women & Nonbinary Network (AWN)United States

    A nonprofit focused on autistic women, girls, nonbinary and trans people, working from a neurodiversity and disability justice perspective.

  • National Autistic SocietyUnited Kingdom

    The UK's main autism charity, with advice across fourteen topic areas from diagnosis and employment to money and relationships, an online community, and an autism services directory.

  • Reframing AutismAustralia

    An autistic-led charity with a free Autistic Welcome Pack and a free Autism Essentials course, plus resources for people who are newly identified or still questioning.

  • AutisticaUnited Kingdom

    A UK autism research and campaigning charity focused on helping autistic people live long, healthy lives.
