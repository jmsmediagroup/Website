Burnout, sleep and energy

On ADHD and autistic burnout, how it differs from depression, hyperfocus crashes, meltdowns, sleep, and managing energy instead of time.

Neurodivergent brains tend to cost more to run. Masking, filtering sensory input, starting tasks through sheer effort, compensating for difficulties nobody else can see: all of it draws on the same limited supply, and most of it does not register as work. That is why burnout so often seems to come from nowhere.

The articles here are about that supply. How to tell autistic burnout from ADHD burnout and from depression, which need very different responses. Why a brilliant hyperfocus day can cost you the rest of the week. Why sleep keeps sliding later, and why a meltdown or a shutdown says something about load rather than character.

The thread running through all of them is that rest only works when it matches the kind of depletion, and that energy is a more useful thing to manage than time. If you are struggling right now, please look at the crisis support listed below, and start with telling the three kinds of burnout apart.

Key terms

Autistic burnout
A period of long-term exhaustion, loss of previously held skills, and reduced tolerance for sensory input, usually caused by sustained masking and demands that exceed capacity over months or years.
Spoon theory
A metaphor in which daily energy is a limited number of spoons, and every task, including small ones, costs some.
Hyperfocus
Intense absorption in a task that is hard to break, often with lost awareness of time, hunger or tiredness.
Revenge bedtime procrastination
Staying up late, even when tired, to reclaim personal time that the day did not allow.
Shutdown
A response to overwhelm in which a person withdraws and becomes less responsive, sometimes temporarily losing the ability to speak or move easily.

Organisations and help

If you need help right now

If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number. These services offer free, confidential support from trained people when things feel unmanageable.

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis LifelineUnited States

    Call, text or chat 988

    Free, confidential support 24 hours a day from trained crisis counsellors, by phone, text or online chat.

  • 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis HelplineCanada

    Call or text 9-8-8

    Free support for anyone thinking about suicide, available 24 hours a day, every day of the year.

  • SamaritansUK and Ireland

    Call 116 123 for free

    A listening service for anyone who is struggling, whatever the reason.

  • LifelineAustralia

    Call 13 11 14

    Crisis support and suicide prevention, with phone, text and online options.

  • 1737, Need to talk?New Zealand

    Call or text 1737

    Free, confidential support around the clock from trained counsellors for anyone in New Zealand.

  • Find A HelplineInternational

    A directory of verified crisis helplines in other countries, searchable by location and topic.

Peer support and body doubling

Talking to people who already understand saves a great deal of explaining. Virtual body doubling is included here because, for a lot of ADHD adults, other people's presence is what makes starting possible.

  • Adult ADHD community (CHADD on HealthUnlocked)International

    A free online peer community for adults with ADHD, run with CHADD.

  • Women with ADHD community (CHADD on HealthUnlocked)International

    A free online peer community for women with ADHD, run with CHADD.

  • FocusmateInternational

    Virtual body doubling: short video sessions where you and another person each work on your own task. There is a free tier.
