ADHD and money

Writing, books and tools on ADHD and money: the ADHD tax, impulsive spending, debt, budgeting that survives a bad month, and earning around your energy.

Money is where ADHD tends to leave the clearest evidence. Late fees on bills you could afford, subscriptions you meant to cancel, impulse purchases you cannot remember wanting, and debt plans abandoned in month three. None of it comes from not caring about money. It comes from the same executive function that ADHD affects everywhere else, applied to a part of life with a running total.

The writing collected here starts from that point. It treats money problems as patterns with causes rather than as failures of character, and it looks for systems that keep working on the weeks when you do not: autopay instead of memory, friction placed where your spending is worst, plans built to restart rather than to be followed perfectly.

If you are new to this, the article on the ADHD tax is the place to begin, and the ADHD Tax Calculator will show you roughly what it is costing you. When you want a budget that allows for it, the free ADHD Budget Template works online or as a spreadsheet for Excel and Google Sheets. For spending you regret, the Impulse Spending Checklist puts a dated wait between wanting and buying. If you are carrying debt, the free not-for-profit services listed below will look at your situation for nothing.

Key terms

The ADHD tax
A community term for the money, and sometimes time, lost to ADHD-related difficulties: late fees on bills you could afford, forgotten subscriptions, replacing things you already own, and paying extra for convenience when the cheaper option had too many steps.
Executive dysfunction
Difficulty with the executive functions: the mental processes used to plan, start, organise, switch between and finish tasks, hold information in mind, and manage time.
Hyperfocus
Intense absorption in a task that is hard to break, often with lost awareness of time, hunger or tiredness.

Organisations and help

Free debt and money advice

Not-for-profit services that will look at your situation for free. If anyone wants a fee to manage your debt, try one of these first.

  • NFCC, National Foundation for Credit CounselingUnited States

    A network of non-profit credit counselling agencies that can help you work out a plan for your debt.

  • StepChangeUnited Kingdom

    A UK debt charity offering free debt advice and fee-free debt management.

  • National DebtlineEngland and Wales

    Free, confidential debt advice by phone, online chat or self-help tools.

  • MABS, Money Advice and Budgeting ServiceIreland

    Free, confidential help with money, debt and budgeting for people in Ireland.

  • National Debt HelplineAustralia

    Free, confidential, not-for-profit financial counselling and step-by-step guides.

  • Credit Counselling CanadaCanada

    A national association of not-for-profit credit counselling agencies.
